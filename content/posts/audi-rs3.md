I bought the RS3 for its five-cylinder engine. To my ears it sounded like a mini V10, and that was the part that separated it from the other fast hatchbacks I could have looked at. I remembered driving it to the Gold Coast and the attention the downshifts drew. 

Mine was pearl white and had 100,000 km. It ran an **ECO Tune Stage 2+ tune making 500 hp and 600 Nm**. The supporting parts included Xforce downpipes, an Integrated Engineering high-pressure fuel pump and a MODE intercooler. Lowering springs and a full Flow Designs aero kit.

## The five-cylinder was the reason

The RS3's 2.5-litre turbocharged five-cylinder gave it an identity that a power figure couldn't fully describe. An odd number of cylinders and their firing sequence produced a distinctive exhaust rhythm. 

At this level, I saw the car as a complete modified package. The attraction was how much performance could sit inside a relatively compact five-door body, with the five-cylinder supplying the character. 

![The engine bay of my RS3](/media/audi-rs3-engine.jpg)

## Putting the performance into a hatchback

What interested me was the packaging. The car kept the basic usefulness of a five-door hatchback while carrying an engine I actively wanted to hear. Compared with the GT-R that replaced it, the rear doors were an obvious practical advantage. Having a daughter made that difference relevant when I thought about what each car asked of me during normal use.

## What I still remember

I can list the modifications on my RS3, but the sound is what comes back to me first. The mid-pipe was my favourite change because it made the five-cylinder louder. I remember the downshifts drawing attention on a drive to the Gold Coast.

## The comfort and the compromises

The sound was what first drew me to the RS3, but there were other things I appreciated once I was living with it. I liked the European touches, especially having heated seats, and I loved how planted the car felt on the road. It had the performance I wanted without giving up the comforts that made it easy to use regularly.

It wasn't perfect. The turning circle frustrated me, particularly for a car that was otherwise so practical. I also found the cost of replacement parts and general upkeep pretty expensive. 

## Why it eventually left

I sold the RS3 to make room for the GT-R. I miss the five-cylinder sound and how planted the Audi felt. I also appreciated the comfortable sports seats and the European comforts. I don't miss its terrible fuel economy or the expensive replacement parts and upkeep. That's the balance of owning it for me: a car I really enjoyed, with drawbacks I remember just as clearly.
