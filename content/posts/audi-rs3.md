I bought the RS3 because of the five-cylinder engine. To my ears, it sounded like a mini V10. That was the reason I wanted one, and it was what I remembered most after selling it.

One drive that stayed with me was a trip to the Gold Coast. The downshifts drew attention. That sound was a big part of why I enjoyed the car, and the mid-pipe was my favourite modification because it made the engine louder.

Mine was pearl white and had 100,000 km on it. It ran an ECO Tune Stage 2+ tune making **500 hp and 600 Nm**. The supporting modifications included Xforce downpipes, an Integrated Engineering high-pressure fuel pump and a MODE intercooler. It also had lowering springs and a full Flow Designs aero kit.

![The engine bay of my RS3](/media/audi-rs3-engine.jpg)

The engine was the reason for the purchase. The extra sound from the mid-pipe was the change I appreciated most. Looking back, that was a useful reminder of what I actually enjoyed spending money on. I could remember the sound on a particular drive much more clearly than a list of parts.

I eventually sold it to make room for the GT-R. I already had the Tesla Model Y, which made that decision easier. I could keep a family car and buy the Nissan I'd wanted for years.

I wanted the RS3 to stay on the site as a car I used to own. It was part of how I got here. Selling it didn't erase that Gold Coast drive or the reason I bought it in the first place.
