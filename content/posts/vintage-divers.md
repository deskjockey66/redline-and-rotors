A dive watch is an exercise in clear priorities. A readable dial. A distinct bezel. A case that protects the mechanism inside. The attraction begins with function, and often ends with something more personal.

## Design with a reason

The photographed Seiko AGS SCUBA dates from 1993. Its dial and bezel communicate at a glance, while the case brings a distinctly utilitarian character. It is an object that belongs to an era without needing to announce the fact.

Vintage watches invite attention to small details: the typography on a dial, the outline of a hand, the meeting of brushed and polished surfaces. Those choices can tell us as much about an object as its brand name.

## Objects with a past

A vintage diver can remain a compelling everyday companion even when its underwater working life is over. Age, seals and service history matter; an old depth rating is no assurance of present water resistance.

Enjoying these watches is about appreciating their design and history. Any return to the water deserves inspection by a qualified watchmaker.
