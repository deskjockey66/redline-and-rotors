I still remember the R35 being unveiled at the Tokyo Motor Show. It was a big departure from the Skylines I'd grown up wanting, and seeing it on Top Gear made me want one even more. I finally bought mine in December 2025, and the first drive gave me goosebumps. After all those years of wanting an R35, it lived up to the expectation.

Mine is gunmetal grey, was imported into Australia in 2025 and had 58,000 km when I recorded its details. It has a custom tune producing **around 650 hp and 700 Nm**, but the power is only part of what appealed to me. The R35's 3.8-litre twin-turbo VR38DETT V6, six-speed dual-clutch transmission, all-wheel drive and rear-mounted transaxle made it a fascinating car long before I owned one.

## The build

NEKO serviced and enhanced the gearbox to handle the extra torque, while a DCL front-mount intercooler and a new oil cooler support the setup. The car also has D1 brakes and black Speed by Design FF5W wheels, measuring **20 × 11 at the front and 20 × 12 at the rear**, with **285/30 front and 335/35 rear tyres**. I particularly like the width at the back, which gives the GT-R so much of its presence on the road.

![My gunmetal grey GT-R from the rear](/media/gtr-rear.jpg)

The bodywork completes the look for me. It has a Top Secret V1 front bar and apron kit with side skirts and rear pods, along with a Top Secret rear wing. I've always liked the factory shape, but the combination of the Top Secret parts, wide black wheels and gunmetal grey paint is how I prefer mine. Its stance is one of my favourite things about the car.

## Living with the GT-R

What surprised me most wasn't the performance but how usable the GT-R is. I've driven it to work, taken it to Cars and Coffee and fitted a child seat in the back for a trip to the Sunshine Coast with my daughter. I can use it for school runs and take the same car to a drag event, then drive home afterwards. I didn't expect a car I'd wanted for its performance to fit into everyday life quite so easily.

The two-door layout does make getting my daughter into her seat more awkward than it was in the RS3, but it hasn't stopped me using the car with her. I sold the Audi to make room for the GT-R, and having the Tesla Model Y as our practical family car made that decision easier. Even so, I've found myself using the Nissan for much more than the occasional drive.

![The rear wing and bodywork on my GT-R](/media/gtr-rear-right.jpg)

## After buying the car I'd wanted for years

I bought the R35 because I'd wanted one since its unveiling, and I still enjoy its road presence every time I see it. The biggest surprise has been how comfortably it moves between ordinary drives and performance events without needing to be treated as a car for special occasions only. So far, I haven't had any disappointments with it.
