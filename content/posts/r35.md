I remembered watching the R35 being unveiled at the Tokyo motor show. It blew me away. Then I saw it on Top Gear and that pretty much locked it into my memory. I wanted one long before I was in a position to buy one.

I bought mine in December 2025. On that first drive, I got goosebumps. After all the time I'd spent watching the car on a screen, I was finally driving my own. I still think that was the most important part of buying it. The power figure mattered, of course, but I'd wanted this car for years.

Mine was gunmetal grey, with a December 2007 build date, so I treated it as a 2008 model. It was imported into Australia in 2025. At the time I wrote this, it had 58,000 km on it.

## I took it to work

I drove it to work and took it to meets, including Cars and Coffee. That was how it became my car rather than something I kept thinking about owning one day. It had a place in the week.

My first JDM car had been an R33 GTST. I remembered its loud aftermarket exhaust and the turbo flutter. That was my starting point. Getting into the R35 years later brought that interest back into focus, with a car I'd wanted since its launch.

I also owned a Tesla Model Y. Having the Tesla meant I could make room for the GT-R after selling the RS3. I didn't need the Nissan to cover every family job before I could justify keeping it.

## The child seat stayed in the back

I fitted a child seat and took my daughter with me. One trip I remembered was taking her to the Sunshine Coast. I liked that she could come along in the car I'd spent so long wanting.

The awkward part was getting her into the seat. Two doors were a limitation. I had to reach into the back and tuck her in, and that was where the shape of the car became a practical compromise. I wouldn't describe it as an effortless family car on the strength of having rear seats.

That didn't stop me taking her. It was simply something I had to allow for. The Sunshine Coast trip belonged in my ownership story just as much as a car meet did.

![My gunmetal grey GT-R from the rear](/media/gtr-rear.jpg)

## What was on this particular car

The bodywork was a Top Secret V1 front bar with the apron kit, including side skirts and rear pods. It also had a Top Secret rear wing. In gunmetal grey with the black wheels, that was the look I'd ended up with.

The wheels were Speed by Design FF5W, measuring 20 × 11 at the front and 20 × 12 at the rear. The tyre sizes were 285/30 at the front and 335/35 at the rear. The brake kit was D1, with eight-piston front calipers and six-piston rears.

The gearbox had been serviced by NEKO. The car also had a front-mounted intercooler and a new oil cooler. On its custom tune, the figures I supplied for this build were **600 hp and 700 Nm**. Those were the figures for my car; I hadn't recorded a separate acceleration test for the journal.

I wanted the details recorded properly because an early R35 with this equipment was a different ownership proposition from a standard car in a launch review. Those parts were already part of the car's story by the time I wrote it down here.

![The Top Secret wing and rear of my GT-R](/media/gtr-rear-right.jpg)

Buying it meant selling the RS3. I had loved that five-cylinder engine, so it wasn't a car I dismissed once the Nissan arrived. But the GT-R had been in my head for much longer. The first drive confirmed how much that mattered to me.

The car I remembered from its unveiling had become the car I drove to work, took to meets and put my daughter's seat in. That was the bit I wanted this journal to keep.
