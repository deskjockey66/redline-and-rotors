I remembered the R35 being unveiled at the Tokyo motor show. It looked like a serious change from the Skylines I'd grown up wanting. Watching it on Top Gear later settled it: this was a car I wanted to own. I bought mine in December 2025, and the first drive gave me goosebumps. That part of the purchase lived up to the years I'd spent thinking about it.

Mine is gunmetal grey. It was imported into Australia in 2025 and had 58,000 km when I recorded these details. It also had a custom tune producing **around 650 hp and 700 Nm**. 

## Why the engineering  interested me

The R35 used a 3.8-litre twin-turbo V6, the VR38DETT, with a six-speed dual-clutch transmission and all-wheel drive. Its rear transaxle arrangement put the gearbox towards the back of the car. The engineering was unusually ambitious for something with rear seats, and that was part of the appeal for me. 

I liked the complete engineering proposition: a turbocharged engine, a fast-shifting gearbox and a driveline designed to distribute the output. 

## The supporting work mattered

NEKO serviced and enhanced the gearbox. With the car tuned to 700 Nm it definitely needs to handle the torque.

## The wheels changed the whole proportion

My Speed by Design FF5W wheels, measuring **20 × 11 at the front and 20 × 12 at the rear**. The tyres are **285/30 front and 335/35 rear**. The rear wheels alone give the car massive presence from the rear.

![My gunmetal grey GT-R from the rear](/media/gtr-rear.jpg)

## More than the power figure

The custom tune makes **650 hp and 700 Nm**, but that number isn't the whole reason I wanted an R35. I remember watching the car on Top Gear years before I could own one. Buying mine in 2025 meant more to me than winning a comparison on paper.

The supporting work is part of the car's story: NEKO serviced gearbox, DCL front-mount intercooler and a new oil cooler. The SBD wheels, wide tyres and D1 brakes are part of it too.

What makes me smile is being able to use it. I've driven it to work, taken it to Cars and Coffee and fitted a child seat for a Sunshine Coast trip with my daughter. 

## The bodywork was part of why I kept looking at it

The Top Secret V1 front bar was paired with the apron kit, including side skirts and rear pods. A Top Secret rear wing finished the bodywork. Those changes suited the broad R35 shape, especially in gunmetal grey. The black wheels kept the appearance tied together without needing a brighter body colour.

I like the factory R35, but I prefer how mine looks with the Top Secret front bar and rear wing.

## Work, car meets and a child seat

The two-door layout was the obvious family compromise. Getting my daughter into the back and tucking her into the seat took more effort than it would in a car with rear doors. The child seat made the trip possible; it didn't make access convenient. 

I also own a Tesla Model Y, which helped make the garage work. I sold the RS3 to make room for the Nissan. The Tesla meant I could keep a practical family option while buying the car I'd wanted for years.

![The rear wing and bodywork on my GT-R](/media/gtr-rear-right.jpg)

## The stance and the everyday surprise

One of my favourite things about the GT-R is its stance. I love the way it sits on the road. The wide wheels, bodywork and overall shape give it a presence that I notice every time I walk up to it. 

What surprised me most was how usable it actually is. I can take it on the school run, drive it to work or take it to a car meet. I can also take the same car to a drag event and drive it home afterwards. I like that I don't need a separate car for the exciting part and another one for the ordinary journey home. The GT-R can do both.

The rear seats aren't as easy to access as those in the RS3, especially when I'm getting my daughter into her child seat. But being able to take her in the car at all is part of why ownership has been better than I expected. The school run and the drag strip sound like two very different uses for a car, yet both fit into how I use mine.

So far, I haven't found anything about owning the GT-R that disappoints me. There are practical compromises in a two-door car, but I knew that when I bought it. The surprise has been how little those compromises have stopped me from enjoying it.

## Where I landed

I waited years to own an R35, and mine has lived up to that anticipation. I love its stance and the presence it has on the road. What I didn't expect was how easily it could switch between school runs and drag events, then get me home again. Getting my daughter into the back takes a little effort, but so far I have no disappointments with the car.
