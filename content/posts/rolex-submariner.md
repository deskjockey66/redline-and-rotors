I bought my Submariner 116610LN from a dealer in Melbourne. It came with its box and papers. For most of this year, it was the watch I wore.

The orange Delugs strap came about because it was summer when I took this photo. I wanted to brighten the watch up. I liked what that colour did to a black-dial Submariner, and changing the strap gave me another way to enjoy a watch I already owned.

## The part I’d change

The thickness annoyed me. I'd have preferred it about a millimetre thinner. That was my main complaint after wearing it, and it mattered more to me than finding another reason to repeat how well known the Submariner was.

I had a similar issue with the Zenith I later sold. With the Rolex, I kept wearing it. With the Zenith, the way it sat on my wrist eventually put me off enough to move it on. Owning both helped me understand that a watch could appeal to me and still have a case I didn't get along with.

I didn't have a dramatic Submariner story to add here. The useful fact was that it kept ending up on my wrist. Over most of the year, I chose it often enough for that to become the story.

The orange strap in the photograph was a summer choice that worked for me. The case thickness was the compromise I kept noticing. Both belonged in the same entry.
